# Optional Activity — Week 2 · Session 1

## Distributed Architectures: Architecture Styles and Candidate Architecture

---

## 1. Official Activity

The Week 2 — Session 1 activity focused on understanding distributed
architecture styles and designing a candidate architecture for the
TeleMed IA project.

The activity required the team to:

- Compare the main distributed architecture styles.
- Analyze coupling, scalability, and failure characteristics.
- Relate service boundaries to business capabilities.
- Identify bounded contexts as potential service boundaries.
- Design a candidate architecture for the product.
- Identify which domains could become services and which should remain
  together.
- Prepare the architectural reasoning that would later be formalized
  during Session 2.

The main architecture styles studied were:

- Client-server.
- Peer-to-peer.
- Layered / N-tier architecture.
- Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA).
- Microservices.
- Event-driven architecture.

---

## 2. Architecture as Boundaries and Communication

An architecture defines how a system is divided into parts and how
those parts communicate.

Two fundamental questions are:

1. How is the system divided into boundaries?
2. How do the different parts communicate?

For distributed systems, these boundaries should preferably be based
on business capabilities rather than only on technical layers.

For TeleMed IA, the business capabilities identified in the project
provide a more meaningful basis for defining architectural boundaries.

---

## 3. Comparison of Architecture Styles

| Architecture Style | Main Idea | Advantages | Main Risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Client-server | Clients communicate with a central server. | Simple communication model and centralized authority. | Possible bottleneck or single point of failure. |
| Peer-to-peer | Each node can act as both client and server. | Decentralization and potential resilience. | More difficult coordination and security. |
| Layered / N-tier | Presentation, logic and data are separated into layers. | Familiar structure and clear technical separation. | Can become tightly coupled or result in a distributed monolith if divided incorrectly. |
| SOA | Business services communicate through service-oriented integration. | Useful for enterprise integration. | Can introduce heavy middleware and centralized coordination. |
| Microservices | Small services organized around business capabilities. | Independent deployment and scalability. | Higher operational complexity and distributed-system failure modes. |
| Event-driven | Components react to asynchronous events. | Decoupling, asynchronous processing and support for workload peaks. | Eventual consistency and more complex debugging. |

The comparison shows that no architecture style is universally
better. The appropriate choice depends on the project's business
boundaries, scale, team capabilities, and operational needs.

---

## 4. Microservices and Business Capabilities

Microservices should be organized around business capabilities or
bounded contexts rather than technical layers.

A microservice should have a clear responsibility and should own the
data associated with its business capability.

For example:

```text
Appointment Scheduling
        ↓
appointment-service
```

The service would be responsible for appointment-related capabilities
such as availability, scheduling, cancellation, rescheduling and
appointment status.

This approach avoids creating services such as:

```text
database-service
controller-service
user-interface-service
```

because those boundaries are based on technical layers rather than
business capabilities.

## 5. Candidate Business Boundaries for TeleMed IA

The TeleMed IA project contains several business capabilities that
can be represented as bounded contexts.

The candidate boundaries are:

| Bounded Context | Main Responsibility | Future Service Candidate |
|---|---|---|
| Identity & Access | Registration, authentication, authorization, roles and permissions | `auth-service` |
| Patient Management | Patient profile and information | `patient-service` |
| Professional Management | Professional profile, license and specialty | `professional-service` |

These boundaries represent business responsibilities rather than
technical layers.

## 6. Candidate Architecture for the MVP

The candidate architecture selected for the MVP is a **Modular
Monolith with Hexagonal Architecture**.

The objective is to maintain strong internal boundaries while avoiding
the operational complexity of deploying multiple independent services
during the initial MVP stage.

A simplified representation is:

```text
                         TeleMed IA MVP
                               |
                       Modular Monolith
                               |
        ------------------------------------------------
        |        |          |          |               |
      Auth    Patient   Professional  Agent      Appointments
        |        |          |          |               |
        ------------------------------------------------
                               |
                       Hexagonal Architecture
                               |
                --------------------------------
                |              |               |
             Domain       Application      Infrastructure
                |              |               |
             Entities       Use Cases       Adapters
             Value Objects  Ports            Database
             Domain Events  Interfaces       External APIs
```

The modules represent business boundaries inside a single deployable
application.

---

## 7. Why Not Start Directly with Microservices?

Microservices provide benefits such as independent deployment and
independent scaling, but they also introduce additional distributed
systems concerns.

These include:

- Network failures.
- Service discovery.
- Distributed tracing.
- Versioned contracts.
- Distributed data.
- Eventual consistency.
- More complex deployment.
- More complex testing and debugging.

For the TeleMed IA MVP, introducing all of these concerns immediately
would increase operational complexity before there is a demonstrated
need for independent scaling or deployment.

Therefore, the candidate architecture follows the principle:

```text
Start with a well-modularized monolith
                ↓
Establish strong domain boundaries
                ↓
Measure real needs
                ↓
Extract services when justified
```

---

## 8. Logical Boundaries vs. Physical Deployment

An important distinction is made between logical and physical
architecture.

The MVP can have several logically separated business modules while
remaining one deployable application.

Logical boundaries:

```text
Auth | Patient | Professional | Agent |
Appointment | Consultation | Notifications | Documents

                    ↓

Physical deployment:

             TeleMed IA MVP
             Modular Monolith
```

This allows the team to establish domain boundaries before introducing
independent deployments.

---

## 9. Future Evolution

The candidate architecture is designed to support future extraction
of modules into independent microservices.

The expected evolution is:

```text
Current MVP
     ↓
Modular Monolith
     ↓
Separated business modules
     ↓
Real scalability or deployment need
     ↓
Selective service extraction
     ↓
Future Microservices
```

The future services would follow the previously identified business
boundaries.

For example:

```text
Appointment Scheduling
        ↓
appointment-service
```

and:

```text
Patient Management
        ↓
patient-service
```

The project therefore does not assume that every module must become a
microservice immediately.

---

## 10. Architecture Trade-offs

The candidate architecture involves explicit trade-offs.

**Advantages**

- Lower operational complexity for the MVP.
- Clear business boundaries.
- Easier local development.
- Simpler deployment.
- Easier initial testing.
- Application of DDD principles.
- Application of Hexagonal Architecture.
- Future possibility of extracting individual modules.

**Disadvantages**

- Modules initially share the same application runtime.
- Independent deployment is not available during the MVP.
- Independent scaling of individual modules is limited.
- Strong discipline is required to prevent unwanted coupling between
  modules.

The architecture is therefore considered appropriate for the current
stage because its benefits match the project's maturity and scope.

---

## 11. Architectural Failure to Avoid

The team identified the risk of creating a **distributed monolith**.

A distributed monolith occurs when an application is split into
multiple services but those services remain tightly coupled through
network calls, shared databases, or synchronized deployments.

For example:

```text
Service A ───REST───► Service B
    │                    │
    └──── shared DB ─────┘
```

This would introduce network and operational complexity without
providing the real independence expected from microservices.

The candidate architecture therefore prioritizes strong modular
boundaries before physical distribution.

---

## 12. Communication Considerations

Different communication approaches may be used depending on the
operation.

Immediate user-facing operations are candidates for synchronous
communication:

```text
Patient
   ↓
Appointment Module
   ↓
Validate request
   ↓
Return result
```

Secondary processes can use asynchronous communication:

```text
Appointment Created
        ↓
   Domain Event
        ↓
Notification Process
        ↓
Notification
```

This supports separation between the primary business operation and
secondary processes.

---

## 13. Candidate Architecture Summary

| Concern | Candidate Decision |
|---|---|
| MVP architecture | Modular Monolith |
| Internal architecture | Hexagonal Architecture |
| Domain organization | Business capabilities / bounded contexts |
| Current deployment | Single application |
| Future deployment | Selective microservices |
| Service boundaries | Based on business domains |
| Database strategy during MVP | Central application database |
| Future service extraction | Only when justified by business or scalability needs |
| User-facing communication | Primarily synchronous |
| Secondary processing | Candidate for asynchronous events |

---

## 14. Activity Result

The activity resulted in a candidate architecture for TeleMed IA based
on business capabilities and bounded contexts.

The team identified the main architectural styles, evaluated their
trade-offs, and selected a Modular Monolith with Hexagonal
Architecture as the appropriate direction for the MVP.

The architecture preserves clear domain boundaries while allowing
future extraction of selected modules into independent microservices
when a concrete technical or business need justifies the additional
distributed-system complexity.
